Estate Planning

Wills, trusts, and planning for the people who depend on you.

Thiru Legal PLLC helps individuals, families, and business owners plan who will manage their affairs if they become incapacitated and how their property will pass after death. We start with what you own, who depends on you, and who you trust to make decisions on your behalf.

Wills

Direct how your property is distributed and who carries out your wishes. We prepare wills that set out who should receive property through your estate and who should carry out your instructions.

  • Property distribution. Identify who should receive specific assets and how the rest of your estate should be divided.
  • Executors and alternates. Name the person you want to administer your estate and an alternate who can serve if needed.
  • Planning for minor children. Nominate a guardian and consider how an inheritance should be managed for children.
  • Will reviews and updates. Review your existing will when your family circumstances, assets, or wishes change.

Trusts

Plan how assets are managed and distributed. We help you decide whether a trust fits your goals and prepare terms that explain who will manage the property, who will benefit, and when distributions can be made.

  • Revocable living trusts. Plan how trust property will be managed during your lifetime, during incapacity, and after death.
  • Trustees and successors. Choose who will manage the trust and set out when a successor trustee takes over.
  • Distributions to beneficiaries. Decide whether beneficiaries should receive assets outright, in stages, or under terms that provide for needs such as education and support.
  • Coordination with your will. Prepare a will to work alongside your trust where appropriate and identify assets to consider transferring into the trust.

Financial Powers of Attorney

Choose someone to handle financial and property matters. We prepare financial powers of attorney that let you designate a trusted person to act on your behalf. We discuss what authority that person should have, when it should begin, and any limits you want to include.

  • Choice of agent. Name a primary agent and an alternate.
  • Scope of authority. Address tasks such as paying bills, managing accounts and property, and signing financial documents.
  • Timing and limits. Consider when the authority should take effect and whether particular decisions should be restricted.

Medical Decisions & Advance Directives

Name a healthcare decision-maker and record treatment preferences. We help you choose someone to make healthcare decisions if you're unable to do so and put your treatment preferences in writing.

  • Medical power of attorney. Designate a healthcare agent and an alternate.
  • Advance directives. Record your treatment preferences for the circumstances covered by your directive.
  • Access to health information. Prepare authorizations for designated people to receive health information where appropriate.
  • Sharing your documents. Discuss which documents to share with your agents and healthcare providers and where to keep signed copies.

Beneficiary Designations & Asset Transfers

Coordinate beneficiary designations, property titles, and trust funding. We review how your assets are titled and who is named to receive them. We help identify where deeds, account records, or beneficiary designations need attention so they fit your overall plan.

  • Beneficiary designations. Review named beneficiaries on retirement accounts, life insurance policies, and other accounts.
  • Property and account ownership. Review deeds and account titles and discuss how they affect your plan.
  • Transfer on Death Deeds. Evaluate whether a Transfer on Death Deed fits your plans for a property and prepare the deed when appropriate.
  • Trust funding. Identify assets to consider transferring to your trust and outline the steps for updating ownership records or working with your financial institutions.

Estate Planning for Business Owners

Coordinate your estate plan with business ownership and succession. We review your estate plan alongside your company's governing documents. This helps clarify who should inherit your ownership interest and who should have authority to manage the business during a transition.

  • Ownership and management. Discuss who should receive your business interest and who should handle company decisions if you become incapacitated or die.
  • Company agreements. Review transfer restrictions, approval requirements, and buy-sell provisions alongside your will or trust.
  • Transfers to family or trusts. Evaluate proposed transfers and coordinate with your accountant or other advisers as needed.
